# Hypotheses shown to the assessors: GABA

Survey section: Dataset 2 — Molecular Regulation of Input Resistance in Developing GABAergic Interneurons

Text exactly as it appears in the survey export; labels A-C are the anonymous labels. The model behind each label is in `../hypothesis_label_mapping.csv`.

## Hypothesis A

This zone captures a differentiating or early post-mitotic CGE-associated neuromodulatory interneuron state marked by low Coro6, high Cnr1, high Htr3a, and low Flt3. In this state, Fxyd6 modulates the Na+/K+-ATPase, stabilizing ionic gradients and resting membrane properties. High Htr3a creates cation-influx potential and a demand for pump-mediated recovery, while high Cnr1 places the cell in a Gi/o-coupled excitability state in which resting conductances are actively regulated. Low Coro6 and low Flt3 mark cells that have moved away from motility- or progenitor-associated programs, making Fxyd6-mediated ion homeostasis more directly relevant to input resistance. Fxyd6 serves both as a marker of this interneuron state and as a functional regulator of the pump-dependent homeostatic mechanisms that shape or stabilize higher input resistance.

## Hypothesis B

This node perfectly describes a well-known post-mitotic CGE-derived CCK-positive interneuron population: Coro6 is low, Cnr1 is high, Htr3a is high, and Flt3 is low. Fxyd6 modulates the electrogenic Na+/K+-ATPase, which becomes a primary driver of resting membrane potential and conductance while developing neurons lack the full suite of mature leak-potassium channels. The receptor-pump coupling hypothesis holds that 5-HT3R and CB1R signaling cascades converge to regulate Fxyd6 activity or its interaction with the Na+/K+-ATPase. In this mechanism, CB1R agonism alters input resistance only when Fxyd6 is present, making Fxyd6 a downstream effector of endocannabinoid signaling during development. Low Flt3 marks a post-mitotic, migrating or maturing window in which Fxyd6-mediated input-resistance regulation is transiently required before mature potassium leak channels such as TASK channels take over resting conductance.

## Hypothesis C

This zone defines developing neurons in which input resistance is maximized and strongly dependent on Fxyd6, with low Coro6, high Cnr1, high Htr3a, and low Flt3 revealing intense neuromodulatory signaling and cytoskeletal quiescence. The scaffold-stabilization hypothesis holds that Fxyd6 acts as a scaffold for Htr3a and Cnr1 heterocomplexes specifically when Coro6-driven actin remodeling is suppressed. Fxyd6 physically links these receptors to the lipid bilayer and prevents their internalization. This stabilization maintains high ligand-gated conductance capacity that paradoxically correlates with high attributed input resistance. This paradox is attributed to the lack of competing Shaker-like potassium leak currents during the same developmental window.
