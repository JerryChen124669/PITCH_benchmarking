## Hypothesis A

The combined pattern—high cytoplasmic Paxillin, high cytobody Paxillin, and high nuclear YAP—identifies a “Mechanical Priming” phase in which high-density focal adhesions coexist with compartmentalized Paxillin bodies. High cytobody Paxillin represents subcellular segregation of adhesion components into cytoplasmic bodies, likely endocytic vesicles or storage granules containing Paxillin. In this state, the actin cytoskeleton is actively remodeled for high-fidelity force transmission, while moderate nuclear YAP may synergize with the pathway and the cytoskeleton provides the structural scaffold for ERK nuclear entry. EGF induces Paxillin-rich cytoplasmic vesicles that retain active Src/FAK signaling complexes away from degradation, enhancing the pool of substrates available for ERK phosphorylation. Mature focal adhesions amplify MAPK/ERK signaling, while Paxillin trafficking or endosomal recycling is a prerequisite for optimal EGF-induced pERK attribution.

## Hypothesis B

The node defines a high-adhesion, high-cytoskeletal-tension, YAP-permissive state in which cytoplasmic Actin strongly predicts pERK: cytoplasmic Paxillin is high, cytobody Paxillin is high, and nuclear YAP is high. Paxillin-rich adhesions provide a scaffold that integrates EGF-EGFR-Ras/Raf/MEK/ERK signaling with Src/FAK-associated adhesion signaling. High cytobody Paxillin represents mature, force-bearing adhesions or central cytoskeletal anchoring sites associated with the strongest Actin-pERK relationship. In this context, cytoplasmic Actin indexes a signaling-competent network that sustains tension, adhesion organization, receptor trafficking, and spatial coordination of EGFR/MAPK signaling. High nuclear YAP marks a mechanotransductively and transcriptionally permissive state that allows EGF-induced ERK activation to be reinforced by actin-paxillin-YAP-associated signaling.

## Hypothesis C

This node defines a tense-scaffold phenotype characterized by high global focal-adhesion presence—high cytoplasmic Paxillin and high cytobody Paxillin—and active mechanotransduction marked by high nuclear YAP. High Paxillin indicates that the cell is firmly attached to the substrate, while nuclear YAP indicates that the actin cytoskeleton is under high mechanical tension. Pre-existing integrin-EGFR crosstalk at these focal adhesions uses the tense actin network to cluster and optimally activate the RAF/MEK/ERK cascade after EGF stimulation. EGF-induced pERK activation is strictly dependent on RhoA/ROCK-mediated actin contractility only when whole-cell Paxillin is high. In this primed state, the tension of the actin network, rather than simply the presence of actin filaments, is the driver of pERK.
