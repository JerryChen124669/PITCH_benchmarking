# Hypotheses shown to the assessors: Ki-67

Survey section: Dataset 3 — PTMA-Centered Regulation of Ki-67 Expression in Breast Cancer

Text exactly as it appears in the survey export; labels A-C are the anonymous labels. The model behind each label is in `../hypothesis_label_mapping.csv`.

## Hypothesis A

The “Hyper-Translational Mitotic” phenotype appears when HNRNPA2B1, TUBB4B, and RPLP0 are all high, creating a transcriptomic microenvironment dominated by RNA processing, ribosome biogenesis, and mitotic structural components. PTMA acts as an upstream chromatin remodeler that drives proliferation-gene transcription, but its effect culminates in Ki-67 expression only when HNRNPA2B1 supplies RNA-processing machinery and RPLP0 supplies massive translational capacity. TUBB4B provides the mitotic-spindle building block required for successful cell division. In this state, PTMA is the ignition, and the HNRNPA2B1/RPLP0/TUBB4B axis is the engine required for successful mitosis in breast cancer cells. PTMA-mediated chromatin unwinding may open the HNRNPA2B1 and RPLP0 promoters, while HNRNPA2B1 may regulate the splicing or export of TUBB4B and MKI67 transcripts.

## Hypothesis B

The co-expression of high HNRNPA2B1, high TUBB4B, and high RPLP0 defines a state of maximal cellular plasticity, with simultaneous splicing, translation, and microtubule-polymerization activity required for rapid division. PTMA acts as a master regulator, potentially a scaffolding protein or RNA-binding element, that stabilizes the co-transcription of splicing factors and cytoskeletal components. High HNRNPA2B1 and RPLP0 couple intense pre-mRNA processing and transcript maturation to immediate import into translating ribosomes. High TUBB4B supplies beta-tubulin for stress-fiber formation and neuronal or epithelial structural integrity before mitosis. PTMA may bind HNRNPA2B1 or TUBB4B promoters or 3-prime untranslated regions to enhance co-transcription, while HNRNPA2B1 may stabilize TUBB4B mRNA.

## Hypothesis C

The Node signature isolates a breast cancer state with three coordinated modules: high HNRNPA2B1-associated RNA processing, high TUBB4B-associated microtubule and mitotic execution, and high RPLP0-associated ribosomal and translation capacity. Within this state, PTMA acts upstream as a chromatin-associated proliferation amplifier that opens or sustains transcriptionally permissive chromatin at MKI67 and other cell-cycle loci. HNRNPA2B1 then supports the processing, export, stability, or splicing of the resulting proliferation-associated transcripts. RPLP0 supplies the ribosomal capacity for protein synthesis, while TUBB4B supplies the microtubule and mitotic machinery needed to execute the proliferative program. Together, these components form a fully enabled proliferation circuit in which transcriptional activation, RNA maturation, translation, and mitotic competence are simultaneously high, stabilizing Ki-67 expression and the cycling state.
